Q2 2026 just delivered three signals. Not from one company. From three. Apple. Google. NVIDIA. Same story. Different angles. 🧵

---

🍎 **APPLE — The Squeeze From Above**

Storage makers are running 60%+ gross margins. Apple, their biggest customer, is getting squeezed from the supply side. This is the classic late-cycle signal: upstream suppliers with pricing power, downstream integrators with none. Apple's pushback is not a negotiation tactic. It's a structural signal that the AI supply chain has hit a friction point.

---

🔍 **GOOGLE — Free Cash Flow. Zero.**

Let that sink in. Google — one of the most profitable companies in human history — just reported FCF of effectively zero in Q2. Two years of AI infrastructure spending caught up.

Here's the brutal part: in 2024, the market read Google's massive capex as "visionary investment in the future." In Q2 2026, the market read the SAME behavior as "where is the return?" Same numbers. Opposite interpretation.

That's an inflection point. Not in the data. In the narrative.

---

⚠️ **NVIDIA — The CDS Market Just Spoke**

NVIDIA's credit default swaps started spiking in late June. And they've accelerated this week.

Why? Because the market is finally pricing NVIDIA's "left foot, right foot" financing model as credit risk. Here's how it works: NVIDIA invests in AI startups → startups use that money to buy NVIDIA GPUs → NVIDIA guarantees the loans → repeat. Confidence high? It's an ecosystem. Confidence low? It's circular debt.

The CDS spike means the market is voting: this is now a credit story, not an innovation story.

---

🧠 **THE PATTERN**

Three companies. Three angles. One inflection.

Same data, opposite narrative.

The AI capex cycle that was celebrated for two years is now being questioned. Not because AI is over — it's not. But because the grace period for spending-without-returns is closing. The market is no longer willing to wait for the killer app. It wants to see the money.

---

⏳ This is normal. Every tech cycle has this moment. The infrastructure build-out runs ahead of the application layer. The bill comes due. The weak hands get shaken out. Then the real winners emerge.

We're at the "bill comes due" moment.
