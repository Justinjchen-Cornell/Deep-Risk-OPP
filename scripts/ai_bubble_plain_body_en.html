The AI Bubble: Risks, the Path, and Second-/Third-Order Opportunities

Put simply, the AI bubble is not “AI doesn't work.” It is “too much compute was built with borrowed money — more than we can use today.” The technology is real. The hyperscalers' profits are real. The problem is that capex is outrunning revenue, and the financing chain is more fragile than the cash flows behind it.

One line to remember

Technology stays; the financing chain breaks. This looks more like 19th-century railways than tulips: when the railway bubble burst, the tracks remained; when tulips burst, nothing remained.

01
HOW IT FORMED

How this bubble formed

Think of AI as a concert everyone is sure will sell out. Tickets sell fast, venues get built at full speed — but whether the seats fill is only known on opening night.

Six steps, one at a time

  • The story is real, and so is the box office. Hyperscalers trade at ~28x earnings with ~25% net margins — not the pure storytelling of 2000. People believe AI is the future, and they pay a premium for it.
  • The arms race begins. Microsoft, Google, Amazon and Meta dare not brake first — whoever cuts capex first is seen as blinking. Rational individually, overinvestment in aggregate.
  • The shovel-sellers get rich first. Nvidia, memory, power, cooling and data-center orders surge. Memory price scales with demand/capacity to the 3.5 power: demand 35% above capacity can lift prices 184%. The three memory makers post record profits.
  • The financing chain lengthens. Once funded from operating cash flow; now project finance, ABS, private credit, supplier finance. Meta's quarterly free cash flow turned negative while it issued non-voting Class C stock — a signal that capex has outgrown operating cash flow.
  • Efficiency gains suppress demand. Anthropic's revenue-per-compute rose from 0.4 to 2.0: the same revenue needs less compute. Nvidia shipments imply ~23GW of global compute, while Anthropic actually uses under 2GW — a compute shortage and idle compute exist at the same time.
  • Credit markets start pricing it. AI-related HY spreads widened 55bp in a week in mid-October; CCC yields hit 15.85%; AI-related CDS weekly volume rose from $2B to $9B; data-center HY bonds fell from 101 to 95. By Oct 30, broad HY OAS is 344bp and CCC 1,327bp (FRED).

The point of these numbers: capex is not the problem — revenue is. You can treat $400B as a bill: it demands $142–370B of AI revenue every year to pay interest and depreciation. If revenue growth stalls, the bill does not disappear; it becomes an impairment.

The gap between supply and usage is idle capacity. 23GW is compute sold to the whole market; <2GW is what one of the largest tenants actually uses. The rest is either rented out at lower prices or sits idle. Falling rents hit data-center cash flow directly.

02
WHERE THE RISK IS

Where the risk actually sits

Not on Microsoft's or Google's balance sheet — downstream first. The giants have cash flow and get hurt last. The first to die are leveraged suppliers, project finance, the memory cycle, and highly levered data-center developers.

LayerWho is most exposedSignals visible now
FirstLeveraged suppliers: second-tier firms that build data centers, buy GPUs, and supply power to the giantsInventory and receivables rising; supplier finance tightening
SecondProject finance / ABS / private credit: highly levered data-center developersData-center HY bonds 101→95; $106B of project finance
ThirdMemory / HBM: Micron, Samsung, SK Hynix3.5-power: demand/capacity 1.35→1.10; prices could fall 51%
FourthHyperscalers: Microsoft / Google / Amazon / MetaStrong cash flow, hurt last; but share prices react early
FifthMacro: high yield, private credit, BDCsCredit contraction → wealth effect → real economy
The hardest signal is credit, not the share price

HY OAS at 344bp and CCC at 1,327bp (FRED, Oct 30); AI-related CDS volume at 4.5x; data-center HY at 95 — real money is pricing default probability. Equities still tell a long-term story; bonds have started calculating whether you can pay.

Hardest signals

HY OAS, CCC OAS, CDS volume, data-center HY bond prices — real money pricing default probability.

Softest signals

“Idle compute” rumors; MIT's 95% of pilots showing no return — direction is useful, but sample and definitions are disputed. Not evidence on their own.

Memory is the most cyclical link in this chain. Prices spike when demand is tight and halve when it loosens. The 3.5-power relationship means profits are very fat on the way up and vanish quickly on the way down. That is why record profits at memory makers may be a sign of a cycle top — not evidence that “AI is forever strong.”

Two memory scenarios

ScenarioDemand/capacityMemory priceMeaning
Tight1.35x+184%Profit peak; record profits at the three memory makers
Loose1.10x−51%Profits disappear; equities and credit both under pressure
How to track memory

Three numbers: contract price (trend), inventory days (supply/demand), HBM mix (structure). Two to three consecutive months of falling contract prices plus rising inventory days is an early cycle-reversal signal; don't wait for reported profits to fall.

HOW IT UNFOLDS

How it unfolds

It will not be “the whole market collapses on one day.” It is a chain: capex growth peaks → suppliers deteriorate → credit cracks first → hyperscalers cut capex → equities de-rate → credit contraction → policy response.

2Inventory and receivables rise; supplier finance tightensInventory days, receivables, supplier financeSecond-tier suppliers
3Credit cracks first: HY/CCC spreads widen, CDS volume jumpsOAS, CDS volume, DC HY pricesHighly levered projects / weak credits
4Hyperscalers cut capex; Nvidia/memory revenue turns negativeNvidia data-center revenue, memory contract pricesChips / memory / equipment
5Equities de-rate; wealth effect and credit contractionS&P, HY default rate, BDC redemptionsRisk assets broadly

GPU rent is the most direct price of data-center cash flow. Falling rents mean lower project revenue, lower collateral value, and harder refinancing; this hits credit earlier than falling share prices do. Watch GPU rents, not the “AI story,” if you want to be close to the truth.

How to think about timing

2026–2027 is the vulnerability build-up; 2028–2030 is the maturity wall. Not “one day in 2026” but a window. You don't need to guess the day; watch the fuel: capex growth, AI revenue, GPU rents, HY/CCC OAS, CDS, utilization.

SECOND-ORDER

Second order: buy contracted bottlenecks, not the hottest chip

First order is AI chips, models and cloud — everyone knows it, and it is the most crowded. Second order is AI's utilities: power, copper, cooling, memory, data centers. The key is not “AI exposure” but orders, contracts, and cash flow.

OpportunityWhyHow to selectWhat to avoid
Power / gridPower is the hardest constraint on AI data centers; transformers, switchgear, transmission, gas turbines and nuclear all benefitOrders, contracts, delivery track record; high service/aftermarket mixCompanies with “AI concept” but no orders
Copper / critical mineralsCopper mines take 17 years to permit and build; supply is rigid. Even if AI slows, grids and electrification continueLow cost, owned mines, production, reservesHigh leverage, single mine, unstable politics
Data-center “landlord” assetsData centers with long-term contracts behave like infrastructure; cash flow is predictableQuality tenants, long contracts, low leverage, low PUEHighly levered developers, single tenant, short contracts
Memory / HBM cycle3.5-power elasticity: fat profits when demand is tight, prices halve when it loosensOnly when demand/capacity is tight and prices rise; watch contract prices and inventoryHolding on as demand/capacity rolls over
Picks and shovelsCooling, power management, testing, certification, fiber — needed whichever model winsAftermarket/consumables/service revenue; diversified customersPure project business, high receivables
The second-order trap

You think you are buying AI; you are actually buying a cycle. Inventory, receivables and customer concentration matter more than the “AI story.” When AI capex growth rolls over, second-order earnings get cut far faster than the giants'.

When to buy

Credit spreads stabilize, capex guidance is not cut, GPU rents stop falling, data-center utilization rises — at least two of these four improving.

When to avoid

"HY OAS widens quickly, DC HY breaks below 90, capex guidance is cut, GPU rents break below $1.5 — two of these four deteriorating.

05
THIRD-ORDER

Third order: after the bubble breaks, buy real assets at a discount

Third order is not “find another AI concept.” It is who ends up with the real assets, cash flow and new demand after the first and second order break. When the railway bubble burst, the tracks remained; compute and power contracts are the tracks.

OpportunityWhyHow to participate
Distressed assets / stressed debtData centers, GPUs and power contracts get bought at discounts; bargains only appear after the bubble breaksDistressed debt funds, special situations, strategic buyers; requires professional diligence
Infrastructure-izationData centers shift from “tech assets” to “infrastructure assets”; the lowest cost of capital winsInfrastructure funds, REITs, power/utilities; focus on long contracts and cash flow
AI implementation services95% of pilots show no return; the opportunity is not the model but implementation, integration, compliance, security and trainingService firms, consulting/integration/security; watch renewals, collections, retention
Risk transfer / insuranceDemand rises for construction completion insurance, AI liability, cyber and credit hedgesInsurance/reinsurance, credit protection; requires underwriting capacity
Sovereign AI / energy securitySovereign compute, defense AI and strategic resources are backed by policy and budgetsPolicy-driven, project-based; watch budgets and delivery, not stories
The key to third order

Not buying at the top, but buying after credit clears, when assets are discounted. It requires cash, patience and expertise. Most people chase first order at the top and dare not buy third order at the bottom — that is precisely where the opportunity is.

07
CONCLUSION

Conclusion: technology stays, the financing chain breaks

AI is not tulips; it is closer to railways. When the railway bubble burst, the tracks remained; when the AI bubble bursts, compute and technology remain. But if you own the leveraged supplier that built the railway, the tracks may remain while you do not.

Seven plain-language conclusions

  • Don't short the giants just because valuation is high. They have cash flow and die last; share prices react early, but fundamentals do not.
  • Watch credit before equities. HY/CCC spreads and CDS are leading indicators; equities are late.
  • Second order: buy contracted bottlenecks. Power, copper, grids, cooling — not companies with a story and no orders.
  • Third order: buy real assets at a discount. Distressed debt, infrastructure, implementation services — after credit clears.
  • Cash is ammunition. A bubble can last longer than you can stay solvent; don't use leverage to bet on timing.
  • Watch the fuel, not the spark. The spark can be any headline; the fuel is capex, revenue, rents, spreads, utilization.
  • Enjoy the technology dividend, but don't become its cost. A bubble is society's tuition for a technological revolution; don't be the one paying it.
“Don't guess the spark; watch the fuel. The spark can be any headline; the fuel is capex growth, AI revenue growth, GPU rents, HY/CCC spreads, CDS volume, data-center utilization, and the 2028–2030 maturity wall.”
One last line

The risk of the AI bubble is not “whether AI fails,” but “at what price, with what money, and over what horizon you choose to believe in it.” The technology stays; the financing chain gets repriced. Only those who survive the repricing get to talk about the next round.
